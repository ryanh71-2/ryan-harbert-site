About working together
Where we begin

When trying harder stops helping

There are things we can understand very well and still find ourselves doing, feeling, or living through again.

It might involve pornography or compulsive sexual behavior. It might be anxiety, depression, a relationship that keeps reaching the same difficult place, or something harder to name. Often there has already been a great deal of effort: therapy, recovery, meditation, advice, resolve. Something remains.

That effort matters. So does the wish for change. We can care about both without making another demand of the part of you that is struggling.

The work

What happens if we listen?

Most of us meet a difficult part of ourselves by trying to understand it, manage it, change it, or get rid of it. I’m interested in what happens when, for a little while, we do something else.

In Focusing, we turn toward what is actually there with a kind of open, bodily attention. This is an active practice, though it does not force an outcome. A little space can make room for something we did not know to come forward. What changes, and when, cannot be prescribed in advance.

Instead of trying harder to change yourself, begin by listening to what is actually there.
