About me

Ryan Harbert

I’m Ryan. I work with men who want to heal from their compulsive behaviors.

My interest in this work comes from my own experience of struggling, seeking help, and finding things that helped. Meditation, spiritual practice, and Focusing have all been part of that.

Focusing has helped me heal, and I still bring it to difficulties in my own life. I know what it is like to want something to change and find that it hasn’t. That experience matters to how I work with people.

Training and experience

I’ve practiced Focusing since 2009. I trained with Ann Weiser Cornell and became certified to teach and guide Focusing through the International Focusing Institute in 2020. I’ve taught individuals and small groups and guided private sessions.

I also trained and became certified as a Progressive Recovery Coach through Michael Pantalon, a psychologist at Yale University. That training focused on helping people with addiction and their loved ones navigate recovery and find appropriate support.

A little more about me

Healing has been a lifelong undertaking for me. I was raised evangelical Christian and eventually left that tradition, but questions about spiritual life, healing, and how we live have stayed with me.

I earned a bachelor’s degree in philosophy with an emphasis in religious studies and a minor in physical science. I’ve spent many years studying different religious traditions and practicing different forms of meditation, including Vipassana, Zen, and Centering Prayer. I’ve also practiced yoga for many years. My own need for healing has been at the heart of these explorations.

Focusing has become a large part of that continuing exploration. I’ve trained in Wholebody Focusing and am continuing that training with Karen Whalen. I also study with Rob Parker and learn from other teachers, scholars, and psychologists working with Focusing and Eugene Gendlin’s ideas. I’m interested in what this work can actually help us with, including the difficulties that don’t readily change.

I’m married and have three children. I think a lot about family life and what helps families be healthy. These are questions I live with in my own family, too.

We’ve spent much of our life together traveling and living in other countries. I appreciate a slower pace of life and learning from people who live differently. When I can, I hike, climb mountains, and make art.

I write here to share what I’m learning and working through, including where I’m still struggling or unsure.

I am a Focusing practitioner, not a psychotherapist. This work can sit alongside therapy or other forms of support.
